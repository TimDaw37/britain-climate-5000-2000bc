Stonehenge, Wiltshire

The winters were like now. They were not a road.

Mid-Holocene Wessex sits in the same maritime temperate world as the modern Plain. A 40-tonne sarsen on the frozen Avon would have needed about a metre of good ice. These millennia do not grow that ice. The Beaker replacement is real. A climate crash that precedes the stones is the wrong order.

Gold’s formula, on the Avon

P = A × h²

Canadian ice-road practice (Gold 1971): load in kilograms, thickness of good floating ice in centimetres, A ≈ 3.5 on a river. For 40 tonnes that is 107 cm. A metre of ice wants thousands of freezing-degree-days. A January whose millennial mean is about 3 °C — the cold end of this reconstruction — is a southern English winter, not an ice road. Lying snow on the downs (Hill 1961) is a different, smaller claim. Pleistocene glaciers carrying stone is a different millennium.

The dates do not make a single story

  1. ~3000 BCStonehenge I. Ditch, first bluestones. Neolithic builders.
  2. ~2500 BCSarsen circle and trilithons. Still before the 4.2 ka label.
  3. ~2450–2400 BCBeaker arrivals. Ancestry replacement begins. Genetic fact; climate not shown as the cause here.
  4. ~2200 BC4.2 ka interval as a global drought label. Roland et al. 2014: no coherent British–Irish peat event. Fenland yew ends in wet / inundation terms, not a freeze.

Three claims, separated

Frozen Avon

Not supported as a planning climate for 20–50 t sarsens. Gold wants a metre. These winters do not supply it.

Crash, then stones, then Beakers

Wrong order. The big stones are earlier than 4.2 ka. Replacement happened. A British climate catastrophe that explains it is not in these series.

Ice-sheet delivery

A Pleistocene argument. These graphs are Holocene climate. They are the wrong tool for that debate.

The strongest British signal in this interval is moisture balance, not temperature. There is no thermometer on Salisbury Plain in the Neolithic. Four millennial slices and a stack of peat, chironomid, oak and speleothem records are what exist.
